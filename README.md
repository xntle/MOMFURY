# MOMFURY

## Summary

Inspired by the legendary slipper throwing prowess of Asian tiger moms. You play as a determined mom fighting to restore order as your home falls into chaos. Armed with her trusty arsenal of household weapons: the Slipper, Rice Machine Gun, and Broom. Mom must face off against waves of household disasters: cockroaches, mysterious poop, and even dreaded A-minus grades! Family members are the real op, they turn into epic boss battles. Can you survive confrontations with Daddy, the rebellious Teen, and even Baby?

## Gameplay Explanation

### CONTROLS

**Movement**

- **WASD** or **Arrow Keys** - Move in 8 directions
- Mom's base movement speed: 50 units/sec

**Combat**

- **Mouse** - Aim your weapon
- **Left Click** - Attack with current weapon
- **Q** - Cycle through weapons (Slipper → Rice Gun → Broom → Slipper...)

**Defense**

- **Space Bar** - Dodge Roll (invincibility frames!)
  - Duration: 0.15 seconds of invincibility
  - Speed: 400 units/sec (8x faster than walking)
  - Cooldown: 0.4 seconds between rolls
  - **CRITICAL**: You are invincible during the roll!

**Menu**

- **ESC** - Pause game / Access menu

---

### WEAPONS ARSENAL

**1. SLIPPER** (Starting Weapon)

- **Type**: Projectile
- **Damage**: 40 per hit
- **Fire Rate**: 0.3s cooldown (fast)
- **Range**: Medium
- **Best For**: Quick, consistent damage against single targets
- **Strategy**: Great all-rounder, perfect for kiting enemies

**2. RICE MACHINE GUN**

- **Type**: Rapid-fire projectile
- **Damage**: 10 per bullet
- **Fire Rate**: 0.1s cooldown (very fast)
- **Range**: Long
- **Best For**: Sustained DPS, controlling crowds from distance
- **Strategy**: Hold down fire and strafe! Excellent for boss fights where you need constant damage output

**3. BROOM**

- **Type**: Melee
- **Damage**: 80 per swing (HIGHEST!)
- **Fire Rate**: 0.5s cooldown (moderate)
- **Range**: Close (hitbox-based)
- **Special**: Knockback effect pushes enemies away
- **Best For**: High damage burst, crowd control
- **Strategy**: Hit-and-run tactics. Swing, then dodge roll away. The knockback creates space for escape

---

### ENEMY TYPES

**COCKROACHES**

- Health: 10 HP
- Damage: 25 contact damage
- Points: 5
- Behavior: Dashes at you after a 0.5s warning pause
- Drop Rate: 20% health pickup
- **Counter Strategy**: Watch for the pause before dash, then dodge roll through them

**POOP MONSTERS**

- Health: 20 HP
- Damage: 8 per bullet (3-shot burst)
- Points: 10
- Behavior: Maintains distance, fires 3-bullet bursts with spread
- Drop Rate: 30% health pickup
- **Counter Strategy**: Strafe between bursts, use Rice Gun to out-range them

**A-MINUS GRADES**

- Health: 80 HP
- Damage: 20 per bullet
- Points: 20
- Behavior: Long-range sniper, keeps far away (300+ units)
- Drop Rate: 50% health pickup
- **Counter Strategy**: Close the gap quickly or use Rice Gun. Priority target due to high HP and damage!

---

### BOSS BATTLES

**BOSS DADDY** (The Beer Thrower)

- Health: 2000 HP
- **Attack**: Throws beer bottles that explode into poison puddles
- **Pattern**: Throws every 1-4 seconds, stops moving while throwing
- **Poison Ground**: Deals damage over time, avoid stepping in it!
- **Strategy**:
  - Circle strafe to avoid bottles
  - Use Rice Gun for safe DPS from range
  - When he's throwing, he's vulnerable - get close with Broom for big damage
  - Watch your footing - poison puddles persist!

**BOSS TEEN** (The Screamer)

- Health: 2000 HP
- **Attack**: Sustained scream beam that follows you
- **Pattern**: 3s beam duration, 5s cooldown
- **Strategy**:
  - When beam starts, RUN perpendicular to it
  - The beam tracks slowly - circle around her
  - Attack during the 5s cooldown window
  - Broom is risky but effective during cooldown

**BOSS BABY** (The Tantrum)

- Health: 2000 HP
- **Attack 1**: Shoots diapers in all 8 directions simultaneously
- **Attack 2**: Summons 5 minions in a circle formation
- **Pattern**: Alternates between abilities randomly
- **Strategy**:
  - For diapers: Move diagonally between the 8 directions
  - For summons: Immediately clear the minions with Broom or focus fire with Rice Gun
  - Most dangerous boss due to summoning - don't let minions stack!
  - Stay mobile at all times

---

### OPTIMAL GAMEPLAY STRATEGIES

**SURVIVAL FUNDAMENTALS**

1. **Never Stop Moving** - Standing still = death. Always strafe while shooting
2. **Dodge Roll Mastery** - Your invincibility frames are your best defense. Use them to:
   - Phase through enemy bullets
   - Escape when surrounded
   - Dodge boss attacks
3. **Health Management** - Enemies drop health pickups. Don't be greedy - collect them before they despawn (3s lifetime)

**WEAPON SWITCHING TACTICS**

- **Slipper**: Default choice for balanced gameplay
- **Switch to Rice Gun when**:
  - Fighting bosses (consistent DPS)
  - Multiple ranged enemies (A-minus swarms)
  - You need to maintain distance
- **Switch to Broom when**:
  - Surrounded by weak enemies (cockroaches)
  - High-value single target nearby
  - Need knockback to create space
  - Boss is stunned/in cooldown phase

**WAVE MANAGEMENT**

- **Early Waves**: Practice weapon switching and dodge timing
- **Mid Waves**: Prioritize A-minus enemies (they're tanky and dangerous)
- **Late Waves**: Keep moving in large circles to group enemies, then Broom sweep or Rice Gun spray
- **Boss Waves**: Learn attack patterns, stay patient, chip away safely

**POINT OPTIMIZATION**

- Cockroach: 5 points (easy kills)
- Poop: 10 points
- A-minus: 20 points (high priority)
- Focus on survival over points - a long run scores more than risky kills

**ADVANCED TECHNIQUES**

- **Kiting**: Keep enemies chasing while you backpedal and shoot
- **Crowd Separation**: Use dodge roll to split enemy groups
- **Safe Zones**: Memorize spawn points and create distance
- **Boss Baiting**: Make bosses waste their attacks on empty space, then punish

---

### WIN CONDITION

There is no final wave - this is **endless survival**! Your goal is to:

- Survive as long as possible
- Defeat as many waves as you can
- Maximize your point score
- Set new high scores for Points and Rounds

**Your high scores are saved in your browser**, so compete against yourself and improve with each run!

---

### QUICK TIPS

Dodge roll has invincibility - use it aggressively!
Broom does 2x damage of Slipper - use it when safe
Keep moving in circles during boss fights
A-minus enemies are the highest priority targets
Don't chase health pickups into danger
Practice weapon switching mid-combat
Learn to dodge roll THROUGH bullets, not away from them

## An Le (xntle)

### Main Role: Game Logic

**Player Movement System** - Implemented 8-directional movement with decoupled movement and animation systems:
- **Movement input**: WASD/Arrow keys build direction vector, normalized (lines 142-158) to prevent √2 diagonal speed advantage
- **Key innovation - Separation of movement vs facing**: Player moves using WASD direction, but animations face mouse cursor position (lines 321-326)
- **Implementation**: `mouse_dir = (mouse_pos - global_position).normalized()` passed to `_get_anim_name()` instead of movement vector
- **Why it matters**: Players expect character to face where they're shooting, not where they're walking - critical for twin-stick shooter feel and strafing gameplay
- **Additional features**: `slow_multiplier` for beer poison debuff, `move_and_slide()` for collision-aware physics
- **Design principle**: Separation of concerns - movement system and animation system are independent, prioritizing player intuition over strict simulation

[Player movement input](https://github.com/xntle/MOMFURY/blob/main/scripts/player.gd#L142-L158) | [Mouse-based animation](https://github.com/xntle/MOMFURY/blob/main/scripts/player.gd#L321-L326)

**Dodge Roll Mechanics** - Created invincibility frame system using collision layer manipulation:
- **Collision layer switching**: Layer 1 (normal, detectable) → Layer 8 (intangible, undetectable) when rolling
- **How invincibility works**: Enemies and projectiles only mask layer 1, so layer 8 player is invisible to them - true invincibility frames
- **Intelligent roll direction**: Uses current input direction if moving, otherwise rolls in `last_move_dir` (last faced direction) - prevents awkward stationary rolls
- **Speed control**: `move_and_collide()` during roll for precise 8x speed boost (400 units/sec vs 50 normal)
- **Cooldown system**: `cooldown_timer` prevents spam, ensures tactical usage
- **Layer reversion**: After roll completes (`roll_timer <= 0`), collision layer reverts to 1 (line 138)
- **Gameplay impact**: High-skill mechanic - players dodge through bullet hell and enemy swarms with precise timing (similar to Enter the Gungeon)
- **Intangibility guardrails**: Lines 227-231 handle multiple invincibility sources without conflicts, checks `if not is_rolling` before reverting layer

[Roll input detection](https://github.com/xntle/MOMFURY/blob/main/scripts/player.gd#L178-L198) | [Collision layer switching](https://github.com/xntle/MOMFURY/blob/main/scripts/player.gd#L138) | [Intangibility management](https://github.com/xntle/MOMFURY/blob/main/scripts/player.gd#L227-L231)

**Weapon Switching System** - Designed OOP weapon hierarchy using polymorphism and state pattern:
- **Type-safe enum**: `enum Weapon { SLIPPER, RICE_MACHINE, BROOM }` (line 11) prevents invalid weapon states
- **Polymorphic interface**: All weapons inherit from `WeaponBase` class with consistent `attack()` method
- **Polymorphic retrieval**: `_get_current_weapon()` (lines 437-445) returns `WeaponBase` reference via match statement - enables uniform weapon interaction regardless of concrete type
- **Cyclic state machine**: `_switch_weapon()` (lines 448-463) implements Slipper → Rice → Broom → Slipper pattern with match/case
- **Visibility management**: Lines 466-474 ensure only `current_weapon` node visible by setting `visible` property
- **Component-based architecture**: Weapons attached as child nodes (`$slip`, `$Ricechine`, `$Broom`) - each self-contained scene with own logic, cooldowns, projectile spawning
- **Polymorphism in action**: `_shoot_current_weapon()` (lines 431-434) calls `weapon.attack()` without knowing concrete weapon type
- **Weapon layering**: Lines 477-489 adjust z-index based on mouse direction - weapons behind player when aiming up, in front when aiming down
- **OOP benefits**: Adding new weapons trivial - create WeaponBase subclass, add to enum, update match statements
- **Course concepts**: Demonstrates inheritance, polymorphism, encapsulation, state pattern

Collision - Notably, I added the collision layers on the map of the game, as well as most of the collision logic the game runs on. I also added functions to apply a stun and apply intangibility to the player to be used in this logic. As the collisionobject of the boss does not have mask 1, this also prevented the disruptive pushing behavior from occurring. I also used extremely similar logic for the cockroach enemy, which attempts to directly collide with the player to do damage. Unlike a boss, it doesn't knock the player away, but rather knocks itself back upon collision. 

Rolling - I also added most of the [roll logic](https://github.com/xntle/MOMFURY/blob/main/scripts/player.gd#L125) for the game. Faithful to other top-down shooters, the roll in the game gives temporary intangibility, allowing you to roll straight through enemies and projectiles. This is accomplished by temporarily changing the players collision layer to 8, which isn't detected by anything in the game except walls. To avoid this logic from interfering with other sources of intangibility(such as the logic ran at the end of a roll ending intangibility from other sources early), there are guardrails in the logic used for other [intangibility sources](https://github.com/xntle/MOMFURY/blob/main/scripts/player.gd#L227) that specifically check if the player is rolling. The logic that turns off intangibility if there is no current intangibility effect, and if the player is not rolling, is also ran continuously to prevent the player from being stuck in layer 8(which would be significantly gamebreaking).

## Main Roles - Animations & Visuals (Quynh Tran)
- As the Animation and Visual Design lead, I was responsible for defining the overall look, motion language, and personality of every character in our game. Much of my work centered on creating 16×24 pixel-art sprite sheets for the player, enemies, and bosses.
  
### Player Animation / State Control)
- My primary technical responsibility was architecting and implementing the complete 8-Directional Animation System for the player character, the "Mom." This involved producing a full suite of sprites—including movement, idle, hit-reaction, and special-state frames—for all eight movement directions. A significant design choice was to use the mouse position to determine the player's facing direction in the [_update_animation loop](https://github.com/xntle/MOMFURY/blob/9de11fa44a8213e075b997d1c3231155001684da/scripts/player.gd#L299). This approach, while technically challenging, enhanced game feel by ensuring that attacking and aiming felt immediately responsive, aligning with core principles of clarity and responsiveness.

- To manage this complex visual system, I built a state machine structure using a Priority Hierarchy of conditional logic. This system ensures that `is_dead` is checked first, followed by `is_hit`, and then `is_rolling`. A crucial challenge was designing the logic within the [_get_anim_name](https://github.com/xntle/MOMFURY/blob/9de11fa44a8213e075b997d1c3231155001684da/scripts/player.gd#L236) helper function to reliably translate the continuous mouse vector into the correct discrete 8-directional animation name using directional vector thresholds. I overcame the initial instability of this approach by refining the thresholds to be more tolerant, ensuring that when the character is actively moving (`direction != Vector2.ZERO`), a corresponding movement animation is always selected, rather than defaulting to `idle_down`. I learned the necessity of creating resilient vector comparison logic when matching continuous input (mouse aim) to discrete visual outputs (8-directional sprites).

### Bosses + Enemies 
- For the boss and enemy animations, I created complete pixel-art sprite sheets that blended visual storytelling with technical precision to support readable, responsive combat feedback. Each character required multiple states—idle, moving, attacking, hit. One of the major technical challenges was maintaining character proportions and alignment across frames, so I built a custom sprite-sheet grid and established anchor rules for feet placement and body pivots. This prevented jittering during playback and ensured smooth transitions between animation states in Godot. To integrate the art into the engine, I sliced the sprite sheets into uniform frames and assigned them to the AnimatedSprite2D node with meticulously labeled animation names so they could be controlled programmatically via state logic in the player and enemy scripts. I also implemented directional variants for bosses and enemies, which required calculating movement vectors and mapping them to eight-direction animation logic.
- The system uses the animation call inside the final animation block (e.g., `anim.play("baby_throw_diaper`) in BossBaby or `anim.play("daddy_attack"`) in BossDaddy) to signal the attack. Furthermore, I implemented the horizontal flipping logic (e.g., `anim.flip_h = true`) based on the boss's movement or facing direction (move_dir or direction_to_player) to ensure the boss always faces the correct direction during attacks and movement, thus improving visual clarity and player readability.

### Hit-Reaction System + Death Animation
- A critical component of this state hierarchy was the Hit-Reaction System, designed for immediate, unambiguous feedback when the player takes damage. I implemented a dedicated "hit" animation state for each movement direction. When the player receives damage, the script sets the `is_hit` flag, overriding all other states and locking the character into the corresponding hit animation for a fixed `hit_duration`. The red color was chosen specifically because red universally signifies damage, danger, or urgency in game design, instantly communicating the state change to the player. This system is an example of Modular Design, abstracting the hit logic away from enemy and weapon scripts.

- The player experience was polished through the implementation of the Death Animation and Post-Death Flow. When the player's health reaches zero, the `take_damage(amount: int`) function sets the `is_dead` flag to true, locks player movement (`velocity = Vector2.ZERO`), and plays the -shot die animation (`anim.play("die")`). Crucially, this function no longer contains the unstable await keyword for audio or the immediate scene change logic. Instead, the transition is managed by the [_on_animation_finished(anim_name: StringName)](https://github.com/xntle/MOMFURY/blob/9de11fa44a8213e075b997d1c3231155001684da/scripts/player.gd#L397) function, which is connected to the animation signal. Upon detecting that the "die" animation has finished, this function initiates a signal-driven asynchronous delay. It does this by creating a persistent `SceneTreeTimer` and connecting its timeout signal to the scene change logic. This reliable use of the timer enforces a crucial two-second pause. Only after this stable delay does the closure execute, loading the End Screen scene (`end_screen.tscn`), passing necessary data (current_points, current_round), and removing the current game scene (`get_tree().current_scene.queue_free()`). This demonstrates effective concepts in Animation-driven game state transitions and Scene management and asynchronous delays, ensuring a smooth, reliable conclusion to the player's run.

### Weapons
- I designed and implemented the visual system for the player's three unique weapons (Slipper, Rice Machine Gun, Broom), focusing on modularity and dynamic rendering. Each weapon is implemented as an individual Godot scene attached to the player hierarchy via dedicated `Node2D` or `Marker2D` nodes (e.g., `$slip`, `$Ricechine`, `$Broom`). This approach follows the Component-Based Architecture principle, ensuring the weapon's visual resources are decoupled from the player's core logic. Weapon selection is managed by dynamically adjusting the visible property of these components to ensure only the `current_weapon` is displayed.
