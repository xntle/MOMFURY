# MOMFURY

## Summary

Inspired by the legendary slipper throwing prowess of Asian tiger moms. You play as a determined mom fighting to restore order as your home falls into chaos. Armed with her trusty arsenal of household weapons: the Slipper, Rice Machine Gun, and Broom. Mom must face off against waves of household disasters: cockroaches, mysterious poop, and even dreaded A-minus grades! Family members are the real op, they turn into epic boss battles. Can you survive confrontations with Daddy, the rebellious Teen, and even Baby?

## Gameplay Explanation

### CONTROLS

**Movement**

- **WASD** or **Arrow Keys** - Move in 8 directions
- Mom's base movement speed: 50 units/sec

**Combat**

- **Mouse** - Aim your weapon
- **Left Click** - Attack with current weapon
- **Q** - Cycle through weapons (Slipper → Rice Gun → Broom → Slipper...)

**Defense**

- **Space Bar** - Dodge Roll (invincibility frames!)
  - Duration: 0.15 seconds of invincibility
  - Speed: 400 units/sec (8x faster than walking)
  - Cooldown: 0.4 seconds between rolls
  - **CRITICAL**: You are invincible during the roll!

**Menu**

- **ESC** - Pause game / Access menu

---

### WEAPONS ARSENAL

**1. SLIPPER** (Starting Weapon)

- **Type**: Projectile
- **Damage**: 40 per hit
- **Fire Rate**: 0.3s cooldown (fast)
- **Range**: Medium
- **Best For**: Quick, consistent damage against single targets
- **Strategy**: Great all-rounder, perfect for kiting enemies

**2. RICE MACHINE GUN**

- **Type**: Rapid-fire projectile
- **Damage**: 10 per bullet
- **Fire Rate**: 0.1s cooldown (very fast)
- **Range**: Long
- **Best For**: Sustained DPS, controlling crowds from distance
- **Strategy**: Hold down fire and strafe! Excellent for boss fights where you need constant damage output

**3. BROOM**

- **Type**: Melee
- **Damage**: 80 per swing (HIGHEST!)
- **Fire Rate**: 0.5s cooldown (moderate)
- **Range**: Close (hitbox-based)
- **Special**: Knockback effect pushes enemies away
- **Best For**: High damage burst, crowd control
- **Strategy**: Hit-and-run tactics. Swing, then dodge roll away. The knockback creates space for escape

---

### ENEMY TYPES

**COCKROACHES**

- Health: 10 HP
- Damage: 25 contact damage
- Points: 5
- Behavior: Dashes at you after a 0.5s warning pause
- Drop Rate: 20% health pickup
- **Counter Strategy**: Watch for the pause before dash, then dodge roll through them

**POOP MONSTERS**

- Health: 20 HP
- Damage: 8 per bullet (3-shot burst)
- Points: 10
- Behavior: Maintains distance, fires 3-bullet bursts with spread
- Drop Rate: 30% health pickup
- **Counter Strategy**: Strafe between bursts, use Rice Gun to out-range them

**A-MINUS GRADES**

- Health: 80 HP
- Damage: 20 per bullet
- Points: 20
- Behavior: Long-range sniper, keeps far away (300+ units)
- Drop Rate: 50% health pickup
- **Counter Strategy**: Close the gap quickly or use Rice Gun. Priority target due to high HP and damage!

---

### BOSS BATTLES

**BOSS DADDY** (The Beer Thrower)

- Health: 2000 HP
- **Attack**: Throws beer bottles that explode into poison puddles
- **Pattern**: Throws every 1-4 seconds, stops moving while throwing
- **Poison Ground**: Deals damage over time, avoid stepping in it!
- **Strategy**:
  - Circle strafe to avoid bottles
  - Use Rice Gun for safe DPS from range
  - When he's throwing, he's vulnerable - get close with Broom for big damage
  - Watch your footing - poison puddles persist!

**BOSS TEEN** (The Screamer)

- Health: 2000 HP
- **Attack**: Sustained scream beam that follows you
- **Pattern**: 3s beam duration, 5s cooldown
- **Strategy**:
  - When beam starts, RUN perpendicular to it
  - The beam tracks slowly - circle around her
  - Attack during the 5s cooldown window
  - Broom is risky but effective during cooldown

**BOSS BABY** (The Tantrum)

- Health: 2000 HP
- **Attack 1**: Shoots diapers in all 8 directions simultaneously
- **Attack 2**: Summons 5 minions in a circle formation
- **Pattern**: Alternates between abilities randomly
- **Strategy**:
  - For diapers: Move diagonally between the 8 directions
  - For summons: Immediately clear the minions with Broom or focus fire with Rice Gun
  - Most dangerous boss due to summoning - don't let minions stack!
  - Stay mobile at all times

---

### OPTIMAL GAMEPLAY STRATEGIES

**SURVIVAL FUNDAMENTALS**

1. **Never Stop Moving** - Standing still = death. Always strafe while shooting
2. **Dodge Roll Mastery** - Your invincibility frames are your best defense. Use them to:
   - Phase through enemy bullets
   - Escape when surrounded
   - Dodge boss attacks
3. **Health Management** - Enemies drop health pickups. Don't be greedy - collect them before they despawn (3s lifetime)

**WEAPON SWITCHING TACTICS**

- **Slipper**: Default choice for balanced gameplay
- **Switch to Rice Gun when**:
  - Fighting bosses (consistent DPS)
  - Multiple ranged enemies (A-minus swarms)
  - You need to maintain distance
- **Switch to Broom when**:
  - Surrounded by weak enemies (cockroaches)
  - High-value single target nearby
  - Need knockback to create space
  - Boss is stunned/in cooldown phase

**WAVE MANAGEMENT**

- **Early Waves**: Practice weapon switching and dodge timing
- **Mid Waves**: Prioritize A-minus enemies (they're tanky and dangerous)
- **Late Waves**: Keep moving in large circles to group enemies, then Broom sweep or Rice Gun spray
- **Boss Waves**: Learn attack patterns, stay patient, chip away safely

**POINT OPTIMIZATION**

- Cockroach: 5 points (easy kills)
- Poop: 10 points
- A-minus: 20 points (high priority)
- Focus on survival over points - a long run scores more than risky kills

**ADVANCED TECHNIQUES**

- **Kiting**: Keep enemies chasing while you backpedal and shoot
- **Crowd Separation**: Use dodge roll to split enemy groups
- **Safe Zones**: Memorize spawn points and create distance
- **Boss Baiting**: Make bosses waste their attacks on empty space, then punish

---

### WIN CONDITION

There is no final wave - this is **endless survival**! Your goal is to:

- Survive as long as possible
- Defeat as many waves as you can
- Maximize your point score
- Set new high scores for Points and Rounds

**Your high scores are saved in your browser**, so compete against yourself and improve with each run!

---

### QUICK TIPS

Dodge roll has invincibility - use it aggressively!
Broom does 2x damage of Slipper - use it when safe
Keep moving in circles during boss fights
A-minus enemies are the highest priority targets
Don't chase health pickups into danger
Practice weapon switching mid-combat
Learn to dodge roll THROUGH bullets, not away from them

## Main Roles - UI

Main Menu - I was responsible for creating the Main Menu. To this end, I created a separate scene with a control root node. This node contains a TextureRect for the splash, as well as VboxContainer containing the buttons to start and quit the game. These buttons all have custom hover and click effects, accomplished by creating different StyleBoxes for the normal, hover and click state. The start button changes the current scene to the game scene. The logic is all contained in [main menu.gd](https://github.com/xntle/MOMFURY/blob/main/scripts/main_menu.gd).

<img width="965" height="540" alt="Screenshot 2025-12-10 at 5 46 26 PM" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/0dc46f38-f244-4a1f-ac97-674c2a629af4" />

Pause Menu - I also created the pause menu, which is mostly derived from the main menu node. To access it, the game checks for a pause input(the Esc button) and toggles the get_tree().paused state. From there, the player can resume by either pressing the input again or clicking on the resume button. This logic is again contained in [pause_menu.gd](https://github.com/xntle/MOMFURY/blob/main/scripts/pause_menu.gd). This pause menu, along with all other gameplay related UI assets, are contained as control nodes within a CanvasLayer subnode of the Game node that is labeled UI. 

Health indicators - I was responsible for creating the underlying logic behind player and enemy health, as well as respective UI indicators for them. Player and enemy health both used an exported float variable for their maximum health as well as a float variable for their current health. The entities are equipped with a take_damage() function([example for the player](https://github.com/xntle/MOMFURY/blob/main/scripts/player.gd#L336)) that both adjusts its health, checks for and runs any death logic, and emits a health_changed signal. This health changed signal(for the player) is then used for the [health label logic](https://github.com/xntle/MOMFURY/blob/main/scripts/health_label.gd) to update the indicated health on the UI accordingly. For bosses, this health_changed signal is used to update their [boss HP bar](https://github.com/xntle/MOMFURY/blob/main/scripts/boss_health_bar.gd). For this health bar's overall function, it is equipped with a connect_boss function that both assigns the associated boss, as well as adjusting its current and maximum health values to match the boss. This connect_boss function is ran in the ready() logic of each boss entity. Accordingly, the boss HP bar shows itself whenever it is assigned to a boss, and also every time its assigned boss takes damage as a guardrail. It automatically hides upon runtime if there is no boss assigned, and also whenever its associated boss dies. Both the player health indicator and boss HP bar use signals to determine when to update its values to save on computing, as opposed to continuously checking these values in a process() or physics_process() function which would be very inefficient. 

## Sub-Roles - Gameplay Testing

As the gameplay tester, I was responsible for adjusting player and enemy balancing to ensure a fun and enjoyable experience. My aim with this role was to create a game that was difficult but felt rewarding to master. From the weapons perspective, my design goal was to ensure that all 3 of the available weapons had a valid use case despite the relative simplicity of our game. I accomplished this by ensuring that the ease of use of each gun was inversely correlated with the raw damage that it offered. Starting with the ricechine gun, it has a base dps of 100(10 damage, 10 shots per second) which was the lowest of the 3 weapons. To compensate it is not only the easiest and least punishing to play, but also the most efficient at dealing with large groups of enemies. With a base damage of 10, it reaches a key threshold of oneshotting cockroaches(the lowest hp enemy in our game), as well as taking poops out in 2 shots. For the slipper, as a slow moving, slow firing projectile it offers a higher dps than the ricechine gun(40 damage with a 0.3s cd resulting in 133.3 dps). This makes it the best choice at dealing with the higher health A minus enemies, whose 80 health threshold allows it to be taken out with 2 slippers. However, the slow movement makes it difficult to use in large areas, requiring precise aim and potential shot leading. Finally, as the broom has the crippling limitation of being a melee weapon, it has the highest dps by far of 160(80 per swing, 2 swings per second). This allows it to oneshot every non-boss enemy in the game, as well as being that weapon that takes out bosses the fastest. However, this is balanced out by the inherent risk of the weapon being melee, which can be extremely dangerous against enemies such as the baby. My design goal with the broom was to create a satisfying, high skill weapon that provides a high risk, high reward option for experienced players. 

I also balanced most of the enemies and bosses in the game, finding a middle ground between challenging and frustrating, while ensuring there was meaningful differentiation between the various enemies. A notable challenge I found was differentiating the A minus enemy and the poop, as their behaviours are extremely similar. I accomplished this by giving them significant stat profile differences. The poop is a lighter, squishier enemy that fires fast, low damage shots and is easily taken out with 2 shots of the ricechine gun or 1 shot of any other weapon. On the other hand, the A minus is significantly tankier, requiring 2 slippers or 8 rice shots, as well as doing heavy damage with their slow projectiles. Ideally, I would give these enemies(alongside the rest of them) different weighting in the spawning algorithm, which is a change I would have liked to implement had we more time to work on the game. I also worked with Kyle on designing some of the boss attacks and ensuring that each of the 3 bosses felt different enough to avoid a stale experience resulting from playing through the endless rounds of the game. To that end, I wrote most of the logic behind the Daddy's beer attack, alongside Kyle, and finetuned its range, speed and damage to make the boss feel engaging and interesting to fight. 

## Other Contributions

I also helped with improving and implementing numerous other systems in the game. 

Collision - Notably, I added the collision layers on the map of the game, as well as most of the collision logic the game runs on. The collision logic is implemented by having separate layers for the player(1), enemies(2), walls(3), player projectiles(4), enemy projectiles(5), transparent walls(used for furniture, 6) and intangible objects(notably the player while rolling, 8). These entities all have masks for walls, as well as any other objects they should detect. In earlier iterations of the game, we ran into many issues where the enemies would push around the player heavily, making movement almost impossible, as both enemies and players used move_and_slide() to move around to ensure consistent behavior with walls. I solved this issue by making the player and enemies not detect each other naturally with their masks, which allows players to pass through enemies without being blocked. To avoid this behavior from happening with bosses(which we deemed unintuitive), I also added [custom logic to bosses](https://github.com/xntle/MOMFURY/blob/main/scripts/Bosses/BossBaby/boss_baby.gd#L322) wherein they would damage players and knock them away if they were touched. I accomplished this by adding an Area2d with mask 1 onto each boss alongside an on_body_entered signal, upon which this logic would run. I also added functions to apply a stun and apply intangibility to the player to be used in this logic. As the collisionobject of the boss does not have mask 1, this also prevented the disruptive pushing behavior from occurring. I also used extremely similar logic for the cockroach enemy, which attempts to directly collide with the player to do damage. Unlike a boss, it doesn't knock the player away, but rather knocks itself back upon collision. 

Rolling - I also added most of the [roll logic](https://github.com/xntle/MOMFURY/blob/main/scripts/player.gd#L125) for the game. Faithful to other top-down shooters, the roll in the game gives temporary intangibility, allowing you to roll straight through enemies and projectiles. This is accomplished by temporarily changing the players collision layer to 8, which isn't detected by anything in the game except walls. To avoid this logic from interfering with other sources of intangibility(such as the logic ran at the end of a roll ending intangibility from other sources early), there are guardrails in the logic used for other [intangibility sources](https://github.com/xntle/MOMFURY/blob/main/scripts/player.gd#L227) that specifically check if the player is rolling. The logic that turns off intangibility if there is no current intangibility effect, and if the player is not rolling, is also ran continuously to prevent the player from being stuck in layer 8(which would be significantly gamebreaking).

## Main Roles - Movement / Physics (Kyle)

*Movement (Enemies and Bosses)* - At first, I was in charge of implementing the movement of each enemy / boss. The very simple and naive way was to use the `move_and_slide()` function to move towards the player's position. This method was used for the first implementations of our game (which is now deprecated). The problem with this method is that as we added different walls and obstacles, the enemy would get stuck trying to move in a straight line towards the player. For the second iteration of the NPC's movement, I used the `NavigationAgent2D`. With this component, we were able to programtically create waypoints that the NPC is able calculate and move to. The direct implementation of creating the way points works in tandem with the `NavigationRegion` that is placed ontop of the tilemap we are using. With this we are able to write a [function](https://github.com/xntle/MOMFURY/blob/main/scripts/Bosses/BossBaby/boss_baby.gd#L95) that will get the best path to the player. After properly testing and completing the movement for singular boss we had at the time, I was able to replicate the process and reuse code / components for other bosses / other enemies. [The Movement Logic](https://github.com/xntle/MOMFURY/blob/main/scripts/Enemies/Aminus.gd#L51)

*Enemy Spawners* - I also built a lightweight spawner system in Godot to control when and where enemy NPCs appear. The spawner itself is stored in a node, and contains an array of spawn locations to put enemies in. We use different control variables to control how many enemies / bosses to spawn at a given time, which is also used to track if the round is over or not. We use this [`_arm_next_spawn`](https://github.com/xntle/MOMFURY/blob/main/scripts/spawner.gd#L100) and [`_on_timeout`](https://github.com/xntle/MOMFURY/blob/main/scripts/spawner.gd#L112) to check whether to spawn another enemy or to hold of on creating a new timer to spawn another enemy. These spawners are used when implementing the round mechanic. Rounds or waves are what cause the game to fully flow properly. This means that users must fight through the rounds to try and get the highest score possible.  Within the spawner, we control the rounds by checking if all enemies are gone and if the number of enemies spawned and the number of enemies killed are equal. Using both this [function](https://github.com/xntle/MOMFURY/blob/main/scripts/spawner.gd) and the `physics_process()` function, we control and use checks to see if the next round should start. For bosses, we first check if the round count is divisible by 3, if it is, we run this [block of code](https://github.com/xntle/MOMFURY/blob/main/scripts/spawner.gd#L60) to spawn a boss at one of the 3 random locations. Lastly, I placed random spawn locations at each room to randomize where each of the enemies will spawn. [Spawner Script](https://github.com/xntle/MOMFURY/blob/main/scripts/spawner.gd)


## Sub-Roles - Game Feel

*Enemy Balancing* - One of my contributions to game feel is how each of the spawners are balanced. This includes wave control, how many enemies spawn in, as well as how hard each of the rounds are. For wave control, each of the waves is controlled by a certain algorithm that scales per wave. Each wave gets increasingly harder and more enemies spawn per round, as seen [here](https://github.com/xntle/MOMFURY/blob/main/scripts/spawner.gd#L52). This simple algorithm is the what I found best for scaling the game, which allows it to scale linearly giving a nice pace of the game.

*Spawn Balancing* - Choosing which locations enemies spawn is one of the other contributions to game feel. This includes choosing strategic locations for enemies to spawn to try and keep the game fair. I decided to place enemies in each room to ensure 2 things. The first is to prevent overcrowding as rounds progress. With the rooms being more spread out, meaning the spawners are spread widely across the map, there is less of a chance for overcrowding and crashing the game. The second thing is that it allows the user to play the game fairly. Since it takes time for enemies to travel to the player, if the player stays near one room, they can optimally balance how many mobs the need to fight at one time. 

## Other Contributions
*Points System* - To make the game have some sort of goal, I worked on obtaining the points for a player, which will then be shown at the end of the game (after death). By implementing a point system I had to assign a point value to each enemy based on how difficult they were to beat. After killing them, it would send a signal to the user to update the "user's points." A part of this logic, we also update the UI on the screen to show how many points the player has accumulated.
